\subsection{Robustness to noise intensity}
\label{subsec:experimental_noise_sensitivity}
Noise sensitivity was assessed for both benchmarks using $10000$ samples and ten data realizations at each innovation standard deviation $\sigma_\nu\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,0.7,1\}$. The nominal initialization $\hat\theta^{(0)}=[0,0,1,0,0,0]^\top$, optimizer settings, and iteration budget were kept unchanged. Within each implementation, matched input and standardized innovation realizations were reused across noise levels. Final-error means and sample standard deviations include all valid finite outputs, including stalled and iteration-limited estimates.

All available PL optimizers return finite estimates throughout the sweep. RLS, WS-GNI, and the diagonal-Hessian GHAM variants exhibit similar, accurate results, whereas WS-GGI and GHAM-2-I have larger low-noise errors under the configured budget. LHAM-3-TIK also improves on LHAM-1-TIK at low noise. FSM methods are more sensitive at high noise: several gradient-root methods reach the AR-noise stability bound and produce repeatable but inaccurate estimates, while both LHAM variants develop large and variable errors. Example~1/FSM GHAM-2-I returns no finite estimates at any tested level. BI-HMC maintains mean errors below $3.3\%$ across both benchmarks, although all saved noise-study fits carry sampler-diagnostic warnings.
